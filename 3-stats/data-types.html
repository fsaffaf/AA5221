📊 Types of Data

There are two main types of data used in research: quantitative and qualitative. Each type serves a different purpose and is used to answer different kinds of questions.

Quantitative data is numerical and can be analyzed using mathematical or statistical techniques (e.g., height, weight, test scores).
Qualitative data is descriptive and focuses on meaning, experience, or perspective (e.g., interview responses, opinions).

While qualitative data helps us understand meaning and context, quantitative data allows researchers to test hypotheses and measure relationships precisely. This course primarily focuses on quantitative data.

Key takeaway: Quantitative = numbers; Qualitative = meaning.

📈 Descriptive vs. Inferential Statistics

Statistics can be divided into two major categories depending on what they are used for.

Descriptive statistics summarize and describe the characteristics of a sample.
Inferential statistics allow researchers to make conclusions about a population based on sample data.

🧍 Sample vs. Population

A population includes the entire group of interest, while a sample is a smaller group taken from that population.

Descriptive statistics describe what is happening in the sample, while inferential statistics allow us to generalize those findings to a larger population.

Key takeaway: We study samples, but we want to understand populations.

📐 Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive statistics help researchers understand the overall structure of their data and make informed decisions about further analysis.

  1. They summarize the data
  2. They help determine which statistical tests are appropriate

Common Measures

Mean: The mathematical average. It is sensitive to extreme values.

Median: The middle value. It is useful when data contain outliers.

Mode: The most frequently occurring value.

Standard Deviation (SD): Measures how spread out the values are around the mean.

Standard deviation helps determine how much variability exists in the data.

Key takeaway: Descriptive statistics tell you what your data looks like.

📏 Levels of Measurement

The level of measurement determines which statistical analyses are appropriate to use.

  1. Nominal: Categories with no order
  2. Ordinal: Categories with a rank or order
  3. Interval: Ordered values with equal spacing (no true zero)
  4. Ratio: Interval data with a true zero
Categorical data: Nominal and ordinal
Continuous data: Interval and ratio
Key takeaway: The type of data determines the type of analysis.
