Types of Data

📊 What is Data?

In research, data refers to the information that researchers collect in order to answer questions. The type of data you collect determines how you analyze it and what conclusions you can draw.

There are two primary types of data: quantitative and qualitative. Understanding the difference between these is essential for choosing the correct research methods.

Key idea: The type of data determines how it can be analyzed.

🔢 Quantitative Data

Numbers Measurement Statistics

Quantitative data is numerical data that can be analyzed mathematically. It allows researchers to calculate averages, compare groups, and perform statistical tests.

Answers: How much? How many? How often?

Examples

  • Height (170 cm)
  • Weight (150 lbs)
  • Test scores (85%)
  • Hours studied

This type of data is essential for testing hypotheses and identifying patterns or relationships between variables.

Key takeaway: Quantitative data = numbers used for statistical analysis.

💬 Qualitative Data

Words Meaning Experience

Qualitative data is descriptive, non-numerical data that focuses on understanding meaning, experiences, and perspectives.

Answers: Why? How? What does it mean?

Examples

  • Interview responses
  • Personal experiences
  • Behavior observations
  • Written feedback

Instead of statistical analysis, qualitative data is analyzed by identifying patterns, themes, and meanings.

Key takeaway: Qualitative data = words that explain meaning and experience.

⚖️ Comparing Data Types

Both quantitative and qualitative data are valuable, but they serve different purposes in research.

Type Focus Use
Quantitative Numbers & measurement Statistical testing, patterns
Qualitative Meaning & experience Understanding perspectives

Many studies combine both types of data to gain a more complete understanding of a research question.

Quantitative = precision | Qualitative = depth
Final takeaway: Quantitative explains what & how much, while qualitative explains why & how.
