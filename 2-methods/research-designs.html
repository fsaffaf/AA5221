Scientific Research

Research Designs

In research, the goal is often to understand whether one variable affects another. However, not all study designs allow us to draw the same level of conclusion. Some designs allow us to say "this causes that", while others can only show that variables are related.

Think of this as a strength scale:
True Experiment → Quasi-Experiment → Correlational Study

STRONGEST True Experiment

A true experiment is the gold standard in research because it allows us to make a strong claim about causality. This means we can say that changes in the independent variable directly caused changes in the dependent variable.

To do this, three conditions must be met:

  • Manipulation: The researcher actively creates different conditions (e.g., high dose vs low dose).
  • Random assignment: Participants are randomly placed into groups, which helps eliminate bias.
  • Control: Everything else is kept as similar as possible between groups.
Example: A researcher gives one group 100 mg of medication and another 50 mg, randomly assigns participants, and ensures all other conditions are identical.

MEDIUM Quasi-Experiment

A quasi-experiment is an "almost" experiment. It includes some, but not all, of the features required for a true experiment.

Most commonly, quasi-experiments are missing random assignment. Because of this, we cannot confidently say that one variable caused another—we can only suggest it.

These are often used when true experiments would be unethical or impractical.

For example, researchers cannot randomly assign people to harmful conditions (like drug use), so they study groups that already exist.

LOW Correlational Study

Correlational studies examine whether two variables are related, but they do not involve manipulation or control.

Important: Correlation ≠ Causation

Even if two variables are strongly related, we cannot assume that one causes the other. There may be a third variable, or the relationship could be coincidental.

Despite being the weakest design, correlational studies are useful because they are fast, inexpensive, and great for early exploration.
