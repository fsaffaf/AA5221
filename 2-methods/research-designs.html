Scientific Research

🧪 Research Designs

In research, the goal is to understand whether one variable affects another. However, not all study designs allow researchers to draw the same kind of conclusions. Some designs allow us to make strong claims about causation, while others can only identify relationships.

Strength scale:
True Experiment → Quasi-Experiment → Correlational Study

STRONGEST 🧠 True Experiment

A true experiment is considered the strongest design because it allows researchers to confidently claim that changes in one variable caused changes in another.

✅ Manipulation of independent variable

✅ Random assignment to groups

✅ High experimental control

All three requirements are present, which allows for strong causal conclusions.

MEDIUM ⚖️ Quasi-Experiment

A quasi-experiment is an "almost" experiment because it includes some, but not all, of the features required for a true experiment.

✅ Manipulation of independent variable (often present)

❌ Random assignment to groups (typically missing)

✅ High experimental control (sometimes present)

Quasi-experiments are missing at least one key requirement, so they cannot fully establish causation.

These designs are often used when true experiments are not possible due to ethical or practical limitations.

LOW 🔗 Correlational Study

Correlational studies examine whether two variables are related, but they do not involve manipulation or control.

❌ Manipulation of independent variable

❌ Random assignment to groups

❌ High experimental control

Because none of the requirements are met, correlational studies cannot determine causation.

Even if two variables are strongly related, we cannot conclude that one causes the other. There may be other explanations for the relationship.

Big picture: The more ✅ a study has, the stronger its ability to show causation.
