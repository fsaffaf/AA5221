Scientific Research

Scientific Research & Research Designs

One of the central goals of scientific research is determining whether one factor actually causes another. Researchers study relationships between variables every day, but not all research designs allow us to draw the same conclusions. Some designs can demonstrate cause-and-effect relationships, while others can only show that variables are associated with one another. Understanding the strengths and limitations of different research designs is essential for interpreting scientific findings accurately. In this lecture, we will compare three major types of research designs—true experiments, quasi-experiments, and correlational studies—and examine how manipulation, random assignment, and control influence the conclusions researchers are able to make.

STRONGEST DESIGN

True Experiment

A true experiment allows the researcher to claim causality. The phrase "claim causality" just means that we can say that the independent variable truly caused the dependent variable.

A true experiment must have these three key features:

Term Definition Example
Manipulation The researcher creates different groups or conditions. Comparing a high-dose medication group to a low-dose medication group.
Random Assignment Participants are randomly placed into groups. Using a random process to assign participants to treatment or control groups.
Control Everything else is kept as similar as possible between groups. Ensuring all participants complete the study in the same environment and under the same conditions.

All three research designs (true experiment, quasi-experiment, and correlational study) are based off of different combinations of these three key features. When trying to determine if a research design is a true experiment, go through this checklist and make sure the design has all three key features:

  • ✅ Manipulation: The researcher creates different groups (e.g., high dose vs low dose).
  • ✅ Random assignment: Participants are randomly placed into groups.
  • ✅ Control: Everything else is kept as similar as possible between groups.

Quasi-Experiment MODERATE DESIGN

A quasi-experiment is the second strongest reserach design. The prefix "quasi" means almost. It is called this because it containes only one or two features required for a true experiment. Because of this, we cannot claim causality when using a quasi-experiment. However, it is still a useful research design. There are many scenarios where the researcher simply cannot assign participants to groups due to nature or ethical reasons. For example, it is not naturally possible to randomly assign participants to be born male or female. Additionally, it is not ethically possible to randomly assign participants to have a traumatic brain injury or not.

Below is one of the most common examples of a quasi-experiment:

  • ✅ Manipulation: The researcher actively creates different conditions (e.g., high dose vs low dose).
  • ❌ Random assignment: Participants are randomly placed into groups, which helps eliminate bias.
  • ✅ Control: Everything else is kept as similar as possible between groups.

Be ware that these are not the only examples of quasi-experiments. There are several others, but it would take up uneccessary space and time to show you all possible combinations.

These are often used when true experiments would be unethical or impractical.

For example, researchers cannot randomly assign people to harmful conditions (like drug use), so they study groups that already exist.

Correlational Study WEAKEST DESIGN

Correlational studies examine whether two variables are related, but they do not involve manipulation or control.

  • ❌ Manipulation: The researcher actively creates different conditions (e.g., high dose vs low dose).
  • ❌ Random assignment: Participants are randomly placed into groups, which helps eliminate bias.
  • ❌ Control: Everything else is kept as similar as possible between groups.
Important: Correlation ≠ Causation

Even if two variables are strongly related, we cannot assume that one causes the other. There may be a third variable, or the relationship could be coincidental.

Despite being the weakest design, correlational studies are useful because they are fast, inexpensive, and great for early exploration.
