Overview

There are two types of statistics: descriptive and inferential.

Descriptive Statistics:
  • Summarize and organize the characteristics of a dataset (means, SDs, frequencies, percentages).
  • Determine whether data are normally distributed to guide inferential test selection.
Inferential Statistics:

Used to test whether the null or alternate hypothesis is supported and to make conclusions about a population based on sample data.

Review From Lecture 1: Writing Scientific Hypotheses

In scientific research, two competing hypotheses are tested:

Null Hypothesis (H₀): No relationship or no difference exists.
Alternate Hypothesis (H₁): A relationship or difference exists.
Design Type Null Hypothesis Alternate Hypothesis
Correlational Relationships No relationship between variables. A relationship exists between variables.
Quasi‑ / True Experiment Group differences No difference between Group A and B. A difference exists between Group A and B.

How Hypothesis Testing Works

  • p‑value
  • alpha (α)
  • statistical significance
  • effect size
P‑Value: The probability of obtaining the observed results if the null hypothesis is true.
Alpha (α): Decision threshold (commonly 0.05).
Statistical Significance: Occurs when p < α.
Result Hypothesis Supported Reporting
p ≥ .05 Null Not statistically significant
p < .05 Alternate Statistically significant
Effect Size: Indicates the strength or magnitude of a relationship or group difference.
  • Report only when results are statistically significant.
  • Statistical significance ≠ practical importance.
