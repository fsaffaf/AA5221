Overview

Before defining what science is, it is helpful to understand what science is not.

Science is Not Personal Feelings

Sometimes we believe something is true simply because it feels true, even when there is no evidence. Feelings are subjective and can be influenced by emotions, expectations, or personal biases. Because of this, personal feelings alone are not a reliable source of scientific knowledge.

Science is Not Personal Experience

Personal experience is limited and can be misleading. We can only observe a small part of the world, and our past experiences often shape how we interpret new events. This influence is called bias.

Science is Not Authority Figures

Authority figures include people or institutions in positions of power, such as parents, teachers, religious leaders, the media, or the government. Science does not accept claims as true simply because an authority figure says they are true. Scientific claims must be supported by evidence that can be tested.

These Ways of Knowing Are Still Useful

Personal feelings, experience, and authority can still be useful in everyday life, even though they are not scientific methods.

Science is the Scientific Method

Science is a systematic way of understanding the world around us.

The scientific method is a structured process for collecting and evaluating evidence to test ideas and answer questions.

The main steps of the scientific method are:

  • Make an Observation
  • Write a Literature Review
  • Form Hypotheses
  • Select Research Study Design
  • Analyze Data & Draw Conclusions

Steps

Step 1: Make an Observation

Every scientific investigation starts with observing something interesting and asking a question.

Step 2: Write a Literature Review

A literature review examines what is already known about a topic.

Step 3: Form Hypotheses

A hypothesis is a testable prediction about relationships between variables.
