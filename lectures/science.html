Overview

Before defining what science is, it is helpful to understand what science is not.

Science is Not Personal Feelings

Sometimes we believe something is true simply because it feels true, even when there is no evidence. Feelings are subjective and can be influenced by emotions, expectations, or personal biases. Because of this, personal feelings alone are not a reliable source of scientific knowledge.

For example, you may meet a person for the first time and feel that they are untrustworthy, even though they have not done anything wrong.

Science is Not Personal Experience

Personal experience is limited and can be misleading. We can only observe a small part of the world and our past experiences shape interpretation. This influence is bias.

Example: believing a jersey causes wins is coincidence, not evidence.

Science is Not Authority Figures

Authority figures can be sincere and confident, yet incorrect. Science requires testable evidence.

Example: gum myth.

These Ways of Knowing Are Still Useful

They help everyday decision making but are not scientific evidence.

Science is the Scientific Method

Science is a systematic way of understanding the world using a structured process.

The scientific method collects and evaluates evidence. Science improves over time.
