Research Design

This lecture introduces the three primary research designs used in scientific research and explains how researchers select the most appropriate design to answer a research question.

Learning Objectives
  • Identify the variables used in research studies.
  • Differentiate between independent and dependent variables.
  • Explain the roles of mediators, moderators, and confounding variables.
  • Describe the three major research study designs.

Before researchers can choose an appropriate research design, they must first identify the variables they want to study. A research design is built around the relationship between variables and determines how evidence will be collected to answer a research question.

Definition: Variable

Variable: Any characteristic or attribute that can take on different values.

Examples include age, income, stress level, dosage, education level, and exam scores.

Primary Variables

Most research studies examine the relationship between two primary variables.

Variable Definition Example
Independent Variable (IV) The variable that is manipulated or used to predict changes in another variable. In the hypothesis, "As exercise increases, blood pressure decreases," exercise is the independent variable.
Dependent Variable (DV) The outcome that is measured to determine whether it changes. Blood pressure is the dependent variable because it is measured after the exercise intervention.

Additional Variables

More advanced studies may include additional variables that help explain, strengthen, or complicate relationships between the independent and dependent variables.

Variable Purpose Example
Mediator Explains how the independent variable affects the dependent variable. Exercise increases endorphins, which then reduce blood pressure.
Moderator Changes the strength or direction of the relationship. The relationship between exercise and blood pressure is stronger for cardiovascular exercise than for weightlifting.
Confounding Variable An uncontrolled variable that provides an alternative explanation for the results. Diet may influence blood pressure independently of exercise.
