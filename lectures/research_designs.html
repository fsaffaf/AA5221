Research Design

Overview

This lecture introduces the three primary research designs used in scientific research and explains how researchers select the most appropriate design to answer a research question.

Learning Objectives
  • Identify the variables used in research studies.
  • Differentiate between independent and dependent variables.
  • Explain the roles of mediators, moderators, and confounding variables.
  • Describe the three major research study designs.

Before researchers can choose an appropriate research design, they must first identify the variables they want to study. A research design is built around the relationship between variables and determines how evidence will be collected to answer a research question.

Definition: Variable

A variable is any characteristic or attribute that can take on different values. Examples include age, income, stress level, dosage, education level, and exam scores.

Primary Variables

Most research studies examine the relationship between two primary variables.

Variable Definition Example
Independent Variable (IV) The variable manipulated or used to predict changes in another variable. Exercise in the hypothesis: "As exercise increases, blood pressure decreases."
Dependent Variable (DV) The outcome measured to determine whether it changes. Blood pressure measured following exercise.

Additional Variables

More advanced studies may include variables that explain, strengthen, or complicate relationships between independent and dependent variables.

Variable Purpose Example
Mediator Explains how the IV affects the DV. Exercise increases endorphins, which reduce blood pressure.
Moderator Changes the strength or direction of the relationship. Cardiovascular exercise produces stronger effects than weightlifting.
Confounding Variable An uncontrolled alternative explanation. Diet affects blood pressure independent of exercise.
Key Takeaway

Every research study begins by identifying variables. Once researchers know what they want to measure, they can select a research design that best answers their research question.

Research Study Designs

A research study design is the structured plan researchers use to collect evidence, measure variables, and answer a scientific research question.

Study Design Primary Purpose Cause & Effect?
True Experiment Determine whether one variable causes changes in another. ✅ Yes
Quasi-Experiment Compare naturally occurring groups. ⚠ Limited
Correlational Study Determine whether variables are related. ❌ No

The Three Features That Differentiate Research Designs

Feature Scientific Meaning Example
Manipulation The researcher deliberately changes the independent variable. Students study for either one hour or two hours.
Random Assignment Participants are randomly assigned to conditions. A computer randomly places students into groups.
Control Everything except the IV is kept constant. Same classroom, instructor, materials, and exam.
Key Takeaway

Manipulation, random assignment, and control determine how much confidence researchers can have in their conclusions.

True Experiment

Definition

A true experiment includes manipulation, random assignment, and control.

A true experiment is the only design that allows researchers to make strong cause-and-effect conclusions.

Key Features

Feature Present?
Manipulation ✅ Yes
Random Assignment ✅ Yes
Control ✅ Yes

Example Research Question

Does studying longer improve exam performance?

Research Component Example
Independent Variable Study time (1 hour vs. 2 hours)
Dependent Variable Exam score percentage
Hypothesis Format

H₀: There is no difference between groups.

H₁: There is a difference between groups.

Quasi-Experiment

Definition

A quasi-experiment includes manipulation but does not include random assignment.

Because participants are not randomly assigned, researchers cannot make the same strong causal conclusions as a true experiment.

Key Features

Feature Present?
Manipulation ✅ Yes
Random Assignment ❌ No
Control ✅ Often

Examples

  • Comparing mothers and fathers.
  • Comparing School A and School B.
  • Comparing people who exercise and those who do not.
Key Takeaway

Quasi-experiments provide useful evidence when true experiments are not practical or ethical.

Correlational Study

Definition

A correlational study measures relationships among variables without manipulating them.

Correlational studies cannot establish cause and effect.

Possible Explanations for Correlations

Possible Explanation Description
Variable 1 Causes Variable 2 One variable influences another.
Variable 2 Causes Variable 1 The direction could be reversed.
Third Variable Explanation An outside variable explains the relationship.
Spurious Relationship The relationship may occur by coincidence.

Key Features

Feature Present?
Manipulation ❌ No
Random Assignment ❌ No
Control ❌ No

Types of Experimental Designs

Design Description
Between-Subjects Different groups experience different conditions.
Within-Subjects The same participants experience every condition.

Final Key Takeaway

True experiments provide the strongest evidence because they allow causal conclusions. Quasi-experiments provide useful evidence when random assignment is not possible. Correlational studies identify relationships but cannot establish cause and effect.
