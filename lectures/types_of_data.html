Types of Data

There are four types of data, also known as levels of measurement. You must correctly identify the level of measurement for your variables because it determines:

  • Which descriptive statistics can be calculated
  • Which inferential statistics can be used
  • Which graphs are appropriate for visualizing the data
Levels of Measurement
Level Definition Examples Data Type
Nominal Categories with no rank order. Race, gender, nationality Categorical
Ordinal Categories can be ranked, but spacing between categories is unequal. Single-item Likert scales Categorical
Interval Equal spacing between values, but no true zero. Multiple-item Likert scales Continuous
Ratio Equal spacing with a true zero point. Weight, height, distance Continuous
Continuous (Quantitative) Data
Number-based data that can be used in higher-level mathematical and statistical calculations.
Categorical (Qualitative) Data
Word-based data that describe categories, groups, experiences, or characteristics.
Single-Item vs. Multiple-Item Likert Measures

Researchers frequently use Likert scales to measure attitudes, opinions, perceptions, and other psychological constructs.

Single-Item Measures

A single-item measure uses one question to assess a variable.

Overall, how satisfied are you with your job?

Advantages: Simple, quick, and reduces survey fatigue.

Limitation: May not fully capture a complex construct.

Multiple-Item Measures

A multiple-item measure uses several related questions to assess a single construct. Researchers typically combine responses using a sum or average score.

  1. I enjoy the work I do.
  2. I am satisfied with my current job.
  3. I would recommend my workplace to others.
  4. My job meets my expectations.

Multiple-item measures are often treated as interval-level data because researchers calculate an overall score across several questions.

Benefits of Multiple-Item Measures
  • More dependable results
  • More accurate measurement
  • Allows evaluation of measurement quality
  • Produces more consistent findings
