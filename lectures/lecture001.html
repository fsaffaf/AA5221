Overview

Before defining what science is, it is helpful to understand what science is not.

Science Is Not Personal Feelings
Sometimes we believe something is true simply because it feels true, even when there is no evidence. Feelings are subjective and influenced by bias and emotion, so they are not a reliable source of scientific knowledge.

Science Is Not Personal Experience
Personal experience is limited and can be misleading. Coincidences can appear causal even when no real relationship exists.

Science Is Not Authority Figures
Scientific claims are not accepted because an authority figure says they are true; they must be supported by testable evidence.

Science Is the Scientific Method

Science: A systematic way of understanding the world through evidence.

The scientific method is an organized process for collecting and evaluating evidence. Science does not claim absolute truth—it improves over time as new evidence emerges.

The main steps of the scientific method:

  1. Make an Observation
  2. Review Existing Research
  3. Form a Hypothesis
  4. Select a Research Design
  5. Analyze Data and Draw Conclusions

Step 1: Make an Observation

Scientific investigations begin with observations that lead to focused questions.

Step 2: Literature Review

  • Summarize what is already known
  • Identify unanswered questions
  • Connect theories to the topic
  • Explain how the study adds new knowledge

Step 3: Form a Hypothesis

A hypothesis is a testable prediction about variables, not something scientists try to “prove.”

Step 4: Select the Research Design

Researchers identify independent and dependent variables, along with mediators, moderators, and confounds.

Step 5: Analyze Data and Draw Conclusions

Statistical tests determine whether evidence supports the null or alternative hypothesis.

Generalizability: The extent to which findings apply beyond the sample studied.

Types of Variables

Independent Variable: Manipulated by the researcher.

Dependent Variable: The measured outcome.

Mediator: Explains how the IV affects the DV.

Moderator: Changes the strength or direction of the effect.

Confound: An uncontrolled variable that influences results.

Research Study Designs

True Experiment

Requires manipulation, random assignment, and high control.

Quasi‑Experiment

Includes some but not all features of true experiments.

Correlational Study

Examines relationships but cannot establish causation.

How to Write Hypotheses

Null Hypothesis: No relationship or difference exists.
Alternative Hypothesis: A relationship or difference exists.
